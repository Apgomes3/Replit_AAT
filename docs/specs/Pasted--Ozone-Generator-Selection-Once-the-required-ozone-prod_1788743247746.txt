### Ozone Generator Selection

Once the required ozone production has been calculated, the application should automatically select the appropriate ozone generator from the PIM product database.

### 1. PIM Product Identification

Ozone generators are classified using the following model format:

**OZ XX**

The XX value represents the nominal ozone production capacity of one unit in **g/h**.

Examples:

**OZ 07 = 7 g/h**

**OZ 11 = 11 g/h**

**OZ 20 = 20 g/h**

The PIM database should contain a dedicated field for:

**Ozone Production Capacity in g/h**

The application should preferably use this numerical database field for calculations rather than relying only on reading the model name.

### 2. Required Ozone Capacity

The ozone calculation provides:

**Required Ozone Capacity, g/h**

For example:

Required ozone capacity = **11 g/h**

### 3. Generator Selection Rule

The total installed ozone generator capacity should be **equal to or greater than the calculated ozone requirement**.

Therefore:

**Installed Capacity ≥ Required Capacity**

The selected capacity should normally not exceed the required capacity by more than **30%**.

Maximum preferred capacity:

**Maximum Preferred Capacity = Required Capacity × 1.30**

For an 11 g/h requirement:

**Minimum Capacity = 11 g/h**

**Preferred Maximum Capacity = 14.3 g/h**

A total installed capacity between **11 and 14.3 g/h** would therefore be considered the preferred selection range.

An undersized system should not be automatically accepted.

### 4. Multiple Unit Selection

The application must be able to use multiple ozone generators when one individual generator cannot provide an appropriate capacity.

Total installed capacity:

**Total Installed Capacity = Unit Capacity × Quantity**

Example:

Required capacity = **18 g/h**

Available generator = **OZ 07**

Using three units:

**3 × 7 = 21 g/h**

Oversizing:

**(21 ÷ 18) − 1 = 16.7%**

Therefore three OZ 07 units would be an acceptable selection.

### 5. Selection Validation

After the generator or generators have been selected, the application should perform a validation calculation.

**Capacity Ratio = Total Installed Capacity ÷ Required Capacity**

And:

**Oversizing Percentage = ((Total Installed Capacity ÷ Required Capacity) − 1) × 100**

The result can then be classified as:

**PASS**

Installed capacity is equal to or greater than required capacity and oversizing is no more than 30%.

**WARNING**

Installed capacity is greater than the requirement by more than 30%.

The system can still show the selection, but it should be flagged for engineering review.

**FAIL**

Installed capacity is below the required ozone production capacity.

### 6. Selection Priority

When several combinations are possible, the automatic selection should prioritize:

1. A solution that meets or exceeds the required ozone capacity.

2. A solution with no more than 30% oversizing.

3. The lowest practical number of ozone generators.

4. The combination with the smallest excess capacity.

For example, if the requirement is 20 g/h and the available options include:

OZ 07

OZ 11

OZ 20

The preferred selection would be:

**1 × OZ 20 = 20 g/h**

rather than:

**2 × OZ 11 = 22 g/h**

or:

**3 × OZ 07 = 21 g/h**

because a single OZ 20 meets the requirement exactly with the lowest equipment quantity.

### 7. Equipment Allocation

Once the selection has been validated, the equipment allocation should be saved against the relevant LSS system.

The saved allocation should include:

Ozone generator PIM Product ID

Ozone generator model

Nominal capacity per unit in g/h

Quantity

Total installed ozone capacity in g/h

Calculated required ozone capacity in g/h

Oversizing percentage

Validation status

Associated FFSP or ozone treatment circuit

The saved equipment allocation should then be available for downstream equipment schedules, BOM generation, costing and design documentation.
