Marine trickling-filter lookup table

For a conservative default, use the Nijhof seawater relationship reproduced and compared in Díaz et al.:

$$ STR=\min\left(0.28,\;0.23\sqrt{C_{\mathrm{TAN,in}}}-0.11\right) $$ $$ VTR=STR\times200 $$

For the Díaz commercial profile:

$$ STR=\min\left(0.64,\;0.49\sqrt{C_{\mathrm{TAN,in}}}-0.24\right) $$ $$ VTR=STR\times160 $$

Here, \(C_{\mathrm{TAN,in}}\) is in mg TAN-N/L, numerically equal to g/m³.

Below approximately 0.25 mg/L, these equations should return “validated rate required”, not zero and not an extrapolated answer.

Full marine MBBR recommendation

For low-TAN public aquarium concept sizing, I recommend a provisional value of:

$$ \boxed{15\ \mathrm{g\ TAN\!-\!N/(m^3\ media\cdot day)}} $$

This is based on marine MBBR commissioning rates of approximately 15–25 mg TAN-N/m²/day with media having 859 m²/m³ total surface area, equivalent to approximately 13–22 g/m³ media/day. The research also demonstrated that nitrite oxidation developed much more slowly than ammonia oxidation. Keuter et al.

This value should be marked:

Concept design only
Marine-adapted mature media required
Manufacturer data or pilot testing required for final design
Separate nitrite-capacity verification required

A freshwater biofilm must never automatically inherit a marine profile. Abrupt exposure of freshwater MBBR media to 25 ppt or more caused complete short-term inhibition in controlled testing. Kinyage et al.

Calculator equation
$$ TAN_{\mathrm{design}}= Feed\times ProteinFraction\times TANCoefficient\times SafetyFactor $$

Using the Losordo convention:

$$ TANCoefficient=0.096\ \mathrm{kg\ TAN\!-\!N/kg\ feed\ protein} $$

Then:

$$ MediaVolume= \frac{TAN_{\mathrm{design}}\times1000}{ApprovedVTR} $$

I recommend keeping the safety factor separate:

1.25 for a validated profile within its operating envelope
1.50 for provisional marine profiles or limited performance data

For MBBR:

$$ ReactorWorkingVolume= \frac{MediaVolume}{MediaFillFraction} $$

The software table should store the published benchmark, approved design rate, TAN range, temperature, pH, salinity, DO, alkalinity, reactor type, media identity, surface-area basis, maturity requirement, source and approval status. This prevents the calculator from treating VTR as a universal property of plastic media.
